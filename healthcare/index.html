Swaasthya Kendra

5,736 people used the Swaasthya Kendra in 2025–26, up from 5,365 the year before.

The centre looks after Madanpur Khadar’s healthcare needs, with general medical services for the whole community: general OPD care, antenatal and postnatal services, infertility-related support, and treatment for reproductive tract infections and other common illnesses, with referrals and health guidance. It is for people who lack easy access to government-run primary health centres, dispensaries and hospitals. Our first community-based health centre was started in 1998–99.

Swaasthya Kendra · health education

Health education, every visit

Awareness is built all the time: during the OPD, medical examinations and immunisation, and in prayer meetings, classes and the assembly hall.

Topics are chosen by what the community needs and its current problems: hygiene, environmental sanitation, food hygiene, hand washing, a balanced diet, and iodine deficiency disorders.

School Health Program

1,192 students of Seth Vidyalaya and Project Savera were given check-ups and OPD care in 2025–26, up from 874.

A medical team of a Medical Officer and an ANM examines every student twice a year: screening for refractive errors, anaemia, dental problems, skin diseases, intestinal worms, ear discharge and night blindness, and assessing mental alertness, general weakness and overall hygiene. The clinic also gives first aid and emergency care to children and staff.

School Health Program · follow-up

A record for every child

Each child has a cumulative health record: health issues, treatment advice and follow-up.

Children who need specialised treatment are referred to hospitals, including AIIMS, and followed up by the medical team. Children with anaemia are advised a protein- and iron-rich diet and given nutritional supplements; children with dental problems are taught to brush twice a day. Children get OPD care and medicines for general weakness, anaemia, colds, coughs and fever, and parents and teachers are given health guidance and counselling too.

School Health Program

Deworming days

848 students and 32 staff, from Nursery to Class V, were given deworming tablets in the campaign.

The clinic also held two awareness sessions for parents: one on waterborne diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera and typhoid, and how hand-washing, clean drinking water and cleaning prevent them; one on health and hygiene, healthy eating and a balanced life for children.
