Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Communities

Care and support for women survivors: 14,958 people reached in 2025–26, in seven communities of South, South East and Central Delhi.

The goal is to reduce gender-based violence (GBV) and to make sure survivors get the services they need. Mahila Panchayats, Crisis Management Centres, Community Resource Centres and Help Desks bring support and information close to home, and help women with government schemes, documents, legal services, healthcare, education, skills and work.

If you or someone you know needs help, call {{phone1}}.

Mahila Panchayat

Ten Mahila Panchayats

245 women in ten Mahila Panchayats settle family disputes and support survivors in their own neighbourhoods.

In Nizamuddin, Sarai Kale Khan, Ashram, Madanpur Khadar (Seth Vidyalaya, A1 and Babloo Dairy), Gautampuri, Shri Niwaspuri, Harkesh Nagar and Okhla Basti, and newly in Makkha Masjid, A1 Khadar, BIW and Double Storey (Ashram). They held 450 meetings this year, learning to support survivors, make safety plans and reach the right services.

Crisis Management Centres

1,303 cases taken up

857 women came to the two Crisis Management Centres and the Mahila Panchayats for help this year.

Together they dealt with 1,303 cases: 319 at the centres and 984 at the Panchayats. 345 were reconciled, and 270 were referred on, most of them to the District Legal Services Authority, and others to the Crime Against Women Cell and police stations.

Community Resource Centres · Nizamuddin and Okhla Phase II

Community Resource Centres

7,565 enquiries about Aadhaar, PAN, E-Shram and other government schemes, and 7,243 people who got the benefits they were entitled to.

The two centres give information and help on government schemes, women’s rights, health and nutrition, including filling in online applications. 69 networking meetings with police stations, dispensaries, the District Legal Services Authority, Anganwadi centres and many other organisations make it easier to refer women on.

Legal Help Desk · every week, in every community

Legal Help Desk

Free legal advice from professional lawyers: 459 people given direct legal help this year.

A Legal Help Desk is held every week in each project community, with help in tracking cases before courts, NGOs and Crime Against Women Cells. 129 legal awareness camps and sessions and 121 help desk sessions reached 4,282 people, on maintenance under the Domestic Violence Act, property rights, compensation in accident cases, divorce, financial fraud and more.

Jagruk Pariwar · aware families

Jagruk Pariwar

100 families in Sriniwaspuri, Ashram, Nizamuddin, Harkesh Nagar and Gautampuri learned about gender equality, gender-based violence and the laws that protect women.

55 follow-up meetings worked towards violence-free zones. Mahila Panchayat and Jagruk members now take up their neighbourhood’s problems together, from rubbish and street lighting to eve-teasing.

Self-Help Groups

Saving, and starting out

11 Self-Help Groups are active, six of them new this year with 75 members.

149 meetings on savings, credit, women-led businesses and community concerns. Women in the groups have saved, started small businesses and taken a bigger part in decisions at home and in their communities.

Project · supported by Samrat Offset Pvt Ltd · since January 2025

Empowering Youth as Agents of Change

153 young people this year, up from 63: 82 girls trained in Beauty Culture & Health Care, and 71 young people in English conversation.

The project grew out of our work against gender-based violence, when girls told us they wanted vocational skills and English. The six-month beauty culture course, on the Shramik Vidyapeeth syllabus, ran at Madanpur Khadar, Okhla and Gautampuri; the three-month English conversation course at Madanpur Khadar, Okhla and Ashram.

Empowering Youth · life skills

Life skills, every week

Critical thinking, communication, emotional awareness and making decisions.

A weekly life skills session for every group builds self-esteem and confidence. Many young people have come forward as volunteers, helping with awareness, documents and work against gender-based violence in their communities.
